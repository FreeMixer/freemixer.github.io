Technical note

"The grouping planner: the fewest workers that pins and history allow"

Given a strip's plugins in signal order, the planner puts consecutive plugins that may share a process into one worker and leaves the others alone, judged by their pins, their cost and what they did on this machine; it stays off by default until the benchmark shows it wins.

The problem

plugin-hostd can run many plugins in one process, which saves CPU and memory. A chain is all-or-nothing, though: a controller declares the members of a strip as one chain, and one member that may not share a process (no qualification verdict, or a verdict of isolated) makes the whole strip run one process per plugin. Nothing is learned from what a plugin did on the machine either.

The planner answers a different question from a chain. A chain is the controller's statement: these run together, in this order. A plan is the daemon's answer to: given these, in this order, which of them may run together. It ends in the chain and add calls the daemon already has; it adds no new way to run a plugin.

The planner

A pure function in plugin-hostd: the same inputs give the same plan, byte for byte, and it reads no clock and no file while it decides.

Inputs: the members in signal order (instance, uri, format); for each, its pin (files, layout, verdict or none), its cost (coreFractionP95 at the daemon's rate and quantum) and latency from the qualification record when there is one, and its history on this host class; and the host class and settings (group_max, group_cost_max, grouping, require_pins, pin_mode).

Output: a list of segments in signal order. A segment is a run of consecutive members that share one worker, or one member alone. A segment of two or more is declared as a chain; a segment of one runs in a worker of its own. Every member left alone gets one reason code.

Segments are runs of consecutive members because a chain is one callback in the declared order, and a member left out of the middle must keep its place in the signal path. So A B C D E with C unfit becomes [A B] [C] [D E].

Rules, in this order; the first that matches a member decides it.

reason when the member
refused pin verdict refused is not added at all (-516)
no-pin no pin, pin_mode strict is not added (-508)
no-verdict a pin without a verdict (a learned pin) runs alone
verdict-isolated verdict isolated runs alone
format-world its format pins only in a world of its own, and the chain's union world cannot be built runs alone
history-crashed the history marks it crashed runs alone
history-xruns the history marks it as raising xruns when grouped runs alone
cost-over its cost alone is over group_cost_max runs alone
format-mixed its program differs from the open segment's and the two do not both run chains in one client starts a new segment
group-full the open segment holds group_max members starts a new segment
group-cost its cost takes the open segment over group_cost_max starts a new segment

A member no rule matches joins the open segment. A plugin with no history is judged by its pin and cost alone: no history is neutral, or a plugin could never earn one. group_cost_max is a fraction of one core; a segment is one callback, so its members' costs add. A member with no known cost counts as the median of the segment's known costs (zero when none is known), and the reasons say cost-assumed.

A member left alone only because it was next to an unfit one may share a pool with such members of other chains. A pool saves resident memory, not wake-ups: each member stays a JACK client. Two chains never share a chain worker.

The verbs

The plan runs in the daemon, so that Zynthian's engine (a Python controller) and OpenMixer's console reach the same answer without carrying the rules.

  • plan <chain> <instance> ... applies the plan: the segments become chains and the lone members run alone. The reply lists the segments as i,i,i;i;i,i. A chain planned again keeps the running workers of segments that did not change.
  • plan_get <chain> reads back the last plan and the reason of each member left alone; a plan_reason event carries the same when a plan changes.
  • plan_clear <chain> gives every member a worker of its own and forgets the plan.

Each segment's worker is pinned to the least-loaded audio core by the placement setting; plan and plan_get report the core of each member.

grouping is a setting, off or on. Under off, plan answers the plan without applying it, with the reason grouping-off.

The history record

What each plugin did on this machine, kept per plugin, per binary and per host class: the key is the uri, the SHA-256 of the first pinned file, and the host class (architecture, core band, clock band). A changed plugin starts a fresh record.

key meaning
first, last the first and last window, UTC seconds
windows_solo, windows_grouped windows run in a worker of its own, or in a worker of two or more
cpu_p95_solo, cpu_max_solo percent of one core over a window, windows run alone only
pss_mb_solo the worker's proportional memory, MiB, largest window run alone
xrun_windows_solo, xrun_windows_grouped windows in which JACK counted at least one xrun
crashes_solo, crashes_grouped worker deaths attributed to the plugin
restarts_grouped worker deaths while it was a member and not the culprit
quarantines times it was quarantined
last_crash, last_crash_grouped UTC seconds

CPU is kept per plugin only for windows run alone: a group's worker time cannot be split among its members honestly. A window is 60 seconds; a window with the plugin up less than half of it is not counted.

The file is $XDG_STATE_HOME/plugin-hostd/history (else ~/.local/state/plugin-hostd/history), mode 0600, written to a temporary file and renamed. Only the daemon writes it, from its idle tick and when the supervisor attributes a death. The planner reads a summary of it: crashed when a crash falls within 30 days, raises-xruns when, after at least 30 grouped windows, the share of grouped xrun windows exceeds the share solo by 0.05.

The history is local. It holds URIs, digests and counters: no audio, no path, no user, session or preset name. The daemon never sends it anywhere, and history_clear deletes it.

Xruns. JACK reports an xrun to a client, not to a plugin, and the daemon holds no JACK client. The worker does, so it reports xruns <count> on its feedback port, and the daemon counts a window's xruns against every member of that worker. A group's xrun window is a suspicion of each member, not proof against one: that is why the planner compares a plugin's grouped share with its own solo share rather than reading an absolute count.

How qualification uses it

plugin-qualify adjust --history <file> writes a second verdict beside the measured one, for the history's host class, with its reason. The measured verdict never changes.

move when reason
in-process to isolated crashed, or raises xruns grouped, on this class history.crashed, history.xruns-grouped
isolated to refused three or more solo crashes within 30 days history.crashed-alone
isolated to in-process the verdict was decided by cost alone, six hours run alone with no crash or restart, and the cost under the profile's budget history.steady

A promotion never lifts a verdict decided by real-time safety, a missing feature, a crash in the prober or an unknown: those are measurements of the plugin, not of this machine. A refused verdict is never promoted. The history can only make the planner more careful than the pin, never less.

In Zynthian

Zynthian's engine sends plan with a strip's members instead of chain when the daemon offers it. One plugin that may not share no longer ungroups the strip, the engine shows the reason of a plugin left alone next to its verdict in the plugin menu, and a restarted daemon makes the same plan from the same inputs.

The benchmark that decides the default

The chains-against-processes scenarios of the omx-bench/1 harness, on a Raspberry Pi 5 and on a desktop, in four modes with the same plugins: individual (one process per plugin), chain (the whole strip as one chain), plan (through the planner, with at least one plugin per strip that may not share) and pool. Each scenario also runs under a fixed stress load: busy processes pinned to the host's cores plus a fixed memory touch.

grouping turns on by default only when, on both machines, at 8 and 16 plugins and every listed rate and quantum, each row repeated at least three times:

  1. without stress, plan has fewer processes than individual, and no more memory, CPU or xruns;
  2. with stress, plan has no more xruns than individual, and fewer over the whole matrix;
  3. plan has no more xruns than chain where a chain is possible, and recovers from a killed worker no slower than chain beyond the measured spread.

If the numbers lose, grouping stays off and the numbers are published anyway.

Failure modes

  • A member crashes: the named culprit is quarantined and moved to a worker of its own, the crash enters the history, and the next plan leaves it alone (history-crashed). A death that names no culprit splits the members; the plan is made again after ten minutes of steady running.
  • Xruns in a group: once the share is over the margin, the next plan leaves the costliest members alone. A plan never changes by itself mid-cycle; it applies at the next declaration, quarantine or plan call.
  • An unreadable history is neither read nor saved, and the planner runs on pins and costs alone (history-unavailable).
  • A worker that does not report xruns leaves the xrun windows at zero, and nothing is marked raises-xruns.
  • A plan the daemon cannot make leaves the members as they were and answers the refusal's code, with the reason plan-refused:<code>.

Technical data

The design of plugin-hostd's grouping, October 2026. Grouping is off by default until the benchmark publishes a win on a Raspberry Pi 5 and on a desktop.
